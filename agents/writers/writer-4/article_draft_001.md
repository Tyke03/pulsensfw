<h2>The Fee Structure: What OnlyFans Takes</h2>
<p>OnlyFans operates on a single flat rate: <strong>20% of all earnings</strong>, deducted before payout. This applies to every revenue stream — monthly subscriptions, pay-per-view messages, tips, and custom content sold through DMs. No tiered plans, no separate processing fees on top. The 80/20 split the platform advertises is accurate and consistent.</p>
<p>Payouts happen weekly every Tuesday, with a $20 minimum withdrawal. In FY2024, OnlyFans processed <strong>$7.22 billion in total fan payments</strong> and retained $1.41 billion as its 20% cut — the other $5.8 billion went to creators collectively. How that $5.8 billion gets divided is where the picture gets uncomfortable.</p>

<h2>What Creators Actually Earn</h2>
<p>The most important number is not the average — it is the distribution. OnlyFans income follows a textbook power law:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Top 0.1%</strong> (~4,600 accounts): Average monthly earnings of ~$146,000. This group takes roughly 76% of all platform revenue.</li>
  <li><strong>Top 1%</strong>: $10,000+ per month, capturing about 33% of total revenue.</li>
  <li><strong>Top 10%</strong>: $1,000–$10,000 per month. Together with the top 1%, this tier accounts for ~75% of all earnings.</li>
  <li><strong>Bottom 50%</strong>: Under $24 per month.</li>
  <li><strong>Overall average</strong>: ~$131/month — skewed upward by the elite tier.</li>
</ul>
<p>Around 300 creators earn over $1 million annually out of 4.63 million total creator accounts. For most people on the platform, OnlyFans generates a side income that barely covers a few bills. The earnings problem is not the platform fee — it is traffic. Creators making real money typically arrived with an existing audience built on Instagram, TikTok, or Reddit, or built one through sustained cross-platform promotion. Estimates for 2026 suggest creators in the top growth tier spend 70% of their time promoting and 30% making content.</p>

<h2>How It Compares: Fansly and Patreon</h2>
<p><strong>Fansly</strong> charges the same 20% as OnlyFans. The key difference is audience size — OnlyFans converts roughly 40% better because of brand recognition and buyer trust. Fansly has stronger built-in content discovery, which matters more for creators without an off-platform following.</p>
<p><strong>Patreon</strong> costs new creators around 10% plus ~3% in payment processing, putting the real cost at about 13%. That is lower than OnlyFans on paper. In practice, Patreon bans explicit content entirely, has no PPV messaging, and does not support custom content sales. Around 90% of high-earning OnlyFans creator revenue comes from those three channels. A lower fee on a smaller total is still less money.</p>
<p><strong>Passes</strong> offers a 90% payout rate (10% fee) and is mobile-first, but is strictly non-nude. For suggestive-but-not-explicit creators, it is worth considering. For most of OnlyFans' adult creator base, it is not an apples-to-apples comparison.</p>

<h2>The Reality for New Creators</h2>
<p>Average monthly creator earnings have dropped from around $150–$180 in 2023 to approximately $131 in 2026, while the total number of creators grew 13% year-over-year to 4.63 million. More creators, same pie, lower average at the middle and bottom.</p>
<p>New creators without an existing audience face the sharpest headwind. OnlyFans does not offer meaningful algorithmic discovery — you are not getting found by browsing. Subscribers need to already know to look for you. For a newcomer, OnlyFans is essentially a payment processor layered on top of whatever promotional infrastructure you have or are willing to build.</p>
<p>Agency arrangements compound the math further: many mid-tier creators work with management that takes 30–40% of gross revenue on top of the platform's 20%. A creator grossing $10,000/month before fees might realistically take home $4,000–$5,000 before taxes and production costs.</p>

<h2>Final Verdict</h2>
<p>OnlyFans' fee structure is clear and not predatory by platform standards — 20% flat, no hidden fees, weekly payouts. What the platform does not provide is discoverability, and that is where most creators fail. The median creator earns around $131/month in 2026. The top 0.1% earns nearly three-quarters of everything. That gap is not closing.</p>
<p>If you already have an audience willing to pay for your content, OnlyFans remains the best-built infrastructure for adult monetization — larger audience than any competitor, more revenue streams, faster payouts. If you do not have that audience, the 20% cut is the least of your problems. Building the audience first is the actual job.</p>
